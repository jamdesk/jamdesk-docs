AI assistants answer questions about your product from your documentation. They can only do that when they can read it. AI Score measures how well they can. Jamdesk scans your published site after every build against the open [AFDocs](https://afdocs.dev) agent-readiness standard. You get a 0-100 score, a letter grade, and a per-issue breakdown.

<Tip>
  **Try it on any site first.** The free [AI Score tool](https://jamdesk.com/ai-score) grades any documentation URL against the same standard. The [AI Score Chrome extension](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/jamdesk-ai-score/mhihkkgpcbmapmojnakhjfhjmiagkhlj) grades sites as you browse. Inside Jamdesk, the score runs automatically on your docs.
</Tip>

## How scoring works

AI Score runs automatically, with no setup. Jamdesk waits for each successful build to go live. Then it scans your published docs and records the result.

- **Runs on every build:** each deploy re-scores your site. A regression shows up the moment it ships.
- **0-100 score and a letter grade** (A+ down to F), measured against the open AFDocs standard. It's the same engine as the free public tool.
- **Re-run any time:** open your project's **AI Score** page and click **Run score now**. It scores the live site again without waiting for a build.

Find it in the dashboard under your project → **AI Score**.

The AI Score page has three parts:

- **Score donut:** your current score and grade, plus the change since your last build.
- **Trend line:** your score across recent builds. A drop shows up at a glance.
- **Category cards:** your checks grouped into seven areas, with a grade per area. Expand one to see its checks and whether they passed, warned, or failed.

A footer notes what was scanned, how many pages were sampled, and the AFDocs spec version.

### What the score measures

Each category groups related agent-readiness checks:

| Category | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Content Discoverability | `llms.txt` and the signals that point agents at your content |
| Markdown Availability | A clean Markdown version of every page for agents to read |
| Page Size & Truncation Risk | Pages small enough to fit an agent's context window |
| Content Structure | Headings, ordering, and content that starts early on the page |
| URL Stability & Redirects | Stable URLs and proper same-host redirects |
| Observability & Content Health | Status codes, caching, and parity between what readers and agents see |
| Authentication & Access | Whether pages are reachable by agents at all |

## Fix issues

Every check that warns or fails has a plain-language explanation. Its fix is written in terms of your MDX and `docs.json`. Findings come in two kinds:

- **Things you fix in your content:** for example, a page whose content starts too far down, an oversized page to split, or content rendered by an interactive component that doesn't appear in the page's Markdown. The fix text names the next step and the pages it applies to.
- **Things Jamdesk does for you:** Jamdesk generates `llms.txt`, Markdown export, status codes, and caching. A failure here usually means a transient build issue. Re-run the score after your next build. Contact support if it persists.

### Copy the fix prompt

Each fixable check has a **Copy prompt to fix** button. It copies a prompt that names the check, the finding, and the fix in your MDX or `docs.json`. Paste it into an AI coding agent ([Claude Code](/ai/claude-code), the [Jamdesk Claude plugin](/claude-code-plugin), [Cursor](/ai/cursor), or [Codex](/ai/codex)) to make the edit. Push to rebuild and re-score.

## Get emailed when your score changes

Jamdesk emails your project members the new score and the most important issue to fix. It sends the email when a build produces your first score, moves your score by 3 or more points, or brings you back up to 100.

- **Toggle it per project:** the **Email team when score changes** switch sits at the top of the AI Score page. It's on by default.
- **Small changes stay quiet:** a one- or two-point move doesn't send an email. Jamdesk compares each new score against the last one it emailed you about. Gradual drift adds up. You get an email when the total change reaches 3 points.

<Note>
  Manual re-runs and build-triggered scores can send the email. Backfilled and public-tool scans never do.
</Note>

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The score says it couldn't read my site">
    A scan comes back empty when your site was briefly unreachable, returned an error, or blocked the scanner. Jamdesk does **not** save these results. A transient blip won't pin a wrong score or dent your trend line. Your last good score stays on the page. Click **Run score now** to try again.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="My score is low because my docs are password-protected">
    AI agents can't read pages behind a password. The score reflects that. A low score is expected when the gate is intentional, as with internal or pre-release docs.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="My project is set to noindex and has no score">
    A `noindex` project has no sitemap for Jamdesk to scan. Jamdesk skips AI Score for it. AI agents can still read your docs through `llms.txt`. It stays published regardless of `noindex`. Remove `noindex` from `seo.metatags.robots` in `docs.json` to get a score again.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A check failed that I can't act on">
    Some checks cover platform behavior Jamdesk owns (`llms.txt`, Markdown export, status codes, caching). These pass by design. A failure almost always means a transient build issue. Re-run the score after your next build. Contact support if it persists.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
